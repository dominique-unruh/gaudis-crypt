                Mass-1 (full probability) lemmas #

                A program p has mass 1 at state σ iff p.wp (fun _ => 1) σ = 1, i.e., the total sub-probability mass produced by p at σ equals 1. This holds for every "real" probabilistic operation in the language (pure, get, set, uniform, …), and is preserved by >>=. The lemmas below let proofs about identical-until-bad analyses and similar mass-conservation arguments compose mass-1 facts cleanly.

                      Identical-until-bad #

                      The "fundamental lemma of game-playing" (Bellare-Rogaway, one-sided form): if two programs p and q agree on every postcondition that vanishes on "bad" outcomes, then p.wp G σ ≤ q.wp G σ + p.wp (G restricted to bad).

                      In our applications, bad is a state predicate (e.g., "the adversary queried chal_x"), p is the original game, q is the simplified "branch-eliminated" game, and G is the win indicator. We get P[p wins] ≤ P[q wins] + P[p triggered bad].
