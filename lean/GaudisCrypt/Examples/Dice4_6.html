    A four-sided die from a six-sided one (EasyCrypt examples/Dice4_6.ec) #

    Roll a six-sided die until it shows 1–4 (D6): each of 1–4 has probability 1/4 (prD6), as for a four-sided die (D4, prD4). The two are related (D4_D6) through EC's theory of rejection sampling, cloned as D4_6: D4 samples the restricted distribution (D4_Sample), D6 is the rejection loop (D6_Sample), and the theory relates the two (D4_6.sampleE_sampleWi, Lib/EC/WhileSamplingFixedTest.lean).

    EC's file, declaration by declaration. All of EC's lemmas are proven.

    Translation:

    • [1..6] is dinter 1 6 (Lib/EC/DInterval.lean); var r <- 5 is the declaration and the assignment; while (5 <= r) r <$ [1..6] has its body in braces.
    • clone WhileSamplingFixedTest as D4_6 with …: the clone; the theory's dt, test at the clone are written WhileSamplingFixedTest.dt, WhileSamplingFixedTest.test.

    Gap list (what keeps the file from reading as EC's):

    • GAP 1 (D4_Sample): EC's rnd asks that the two distributions give each value the same mass, which the script computes (dexcepted1E, weight_dinter, dinter1E, dinterE, iotaS, …); equiv_rnd asks that they are equal, which is the library's dinter_dexcepted (an interval conditioned on a sub-interval).
    • GAP 2 (D4_D6): EC's proc * and rewrite equiv[{1} 1 L args] (the call on the left replaced along an equivalence, three times, then sim) are two transitivity steps on the procedures (equiv_trans (N.f) (P₁ ==> Q₁) (P₂ ==> Q₂)): through D4_6.SampleE.sample (D4_Sample) and D4_6.SampleWi.sample (D4_6.sampleE_sampleWi, then D6_Sample reversed). The argument ((), 5 :@ r) and the side condition dinter_ll of EC's second rewrite are the hP goal of the second step.
    • GAP 3: rnd f finv; auto=> /> is equiv_rnd f finv; equiv_skip (equiv_auto loops on b = f (finv b)); EC's rewrite supp_dinter /# on the conditions (and apply: dinter_uni) are smt_close [supp_dinter], smt_close [supp_dinter, dinter1E].
    • GAP 4: byphoare (: True ==> _) for byphoare=> //; rnd on an exact bound is phoare_rnd (pred1 k).
