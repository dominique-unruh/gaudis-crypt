    Handle.eca's CF_G1 in EC's form — the tactic experiment #

    EC's CF_G1 (the game-level equivalence of CF and G1 up to the bad events, through EC's up-to-bad call (_: bad, inv, inv') over the distinguisher's three oracle procedures), stated and proven as EC does, over the old port's declarations (section FirstPort below). It drove the tactics the SmartCounter files need (notes/SHA3/Refactor.md, phase 3): equiv_call (_ : bad, I, J), glob D in conditions, equiv_conseq H, phoare_while I V, sp keeping the precondition, glob_simp. The declarations move to EC's form with the port of SLCommon, ConcreteF, Handle.

    Deviations from EC's proof:

    • the oracles' steps eq_f, eq_fi, PFf_Cf are the old port's (with its footprint hypothesis for D, discharged by fp_disjoint), and so is INV_CF_G1_init (EC's 18 smt calls);
    • the right oracles' losslessness is one more goal each (exception E11);
    • EC splits the right C.f's judgment in a hoare (bad kept) and a lossless phoare (conseq (: _ ==> true) (: _ ==> G1.bcol \/ G1.bext)): here one phoare_while with the bad event and J in the invariant;
    • EC's closers (smt, /#, progress) are simp, split_ifs, omega.

    The first port's translation notes (some mention declarations that went with its proofs):

    • clone export PROM.FullRO as ROhandle with type in_t <- handle, type out_t <- capacity … is the namespace ROhandle; its exported FRO.m is the alias FRO.m here.
    • clone export ConcreteF as ConcreteF1 clones a theory without parameters: it is ConcreteF itself, opened.
    • G1's inner modules M, S do not depend on D: they are the modules G1.M, G1.S. G1 D is the game, with D a Lean parameter.
    • The equivalences relate procedures of modules: they are prhlProc statements, with the section's declare module D and its restrictions as hypotheses.
