    Two more forms of EasyCrypt's conseq #

    With a Hoare triple about one side #

    equiv_conseq (: P ==> Q) _ (: P₂ ==> Q₂)     -- EC's `conseq (_ : P ==> Q) _ (_ : P₂ ==> Q₂)`

    On a relational judgment c₁ ~ c₂ : A ==> B: what the right program guarantees on its own (hoare[ c₂ : P₂ ==> Q₂ ]) is proved apart from the equivalence. P₂, Q₂ are about the right memory and are written with its tag (size §bs{2} = §n{2}, where EC writes size bs = n): a condition without tags could not name a variable that only the judgment's conditions read.

    Goals in EC's order: pre (A implies P and P₂), post (EC's A => (frames) => Q => Q₂ => B, as equiv_conseq (: P ==> Q) states it), hoare (the triple about c₂), judgment (c₁ ~ c₂ : P ==> Q); _ keeps a condition of the judgment. The rule is prhlStmt_and_hoare_right under prhlStmt_conseq_frame.

    conseq H on procedures, the precondition kept #

    equiv_conseq_pre H                            -- EC's `conseq H` on `equiv[ f ~ g : P ==> Q ]`

    equiv_conseq H states post as Q' ⇒ Q alone. EC states it under the precondition (forall &1 &2, P => forall res…, Q' => Q), which a proof may need (a fact about the arguments). equiv_conseq_pre H leaves pre (P ⇒ P') and that post: for the arguments and the initial globals that satisfy P, and every result and final globals, Q' ⇒ Q. The rule is prhlProc_conseq_pre.
