    Swapping statements (EC's swap) #

    Two adjacent statements can be exchanged when neither sees the other's effect. Two cases cover what game hops need:

    • a deterministic statement d (Stmt.Det d u: it runs as the update u) and a statement s that commutes with u (Stmt.CommutesWith s u): d; s ≡ s; d (Stmt.swap_det);
    • a sampling x <$ e from a distribution that does not depend on the state, and a statement that commutes with every write to x: x <$ e; s ≡ s; x <$ e (Stmt.swap_sample, by Fubini for the two independent draws).

    Stmt.CommutesWith s u — running s from u σ is running it from σ and applying u after — is stated on expectations, and proved syntax-directed (assignments, samplings, calls, conditionals, sequences) from state equations that simp settles for distinct variables: the update leaves what s reads alone, and commutes with what s writes. A call commutes with an update of the caller's locals that does not touch the result variable or the arguments: the callee runs on its own locals.

    Like split and inline, a swap is an equivalence of statements, so it works on every registered judgment (stmt_swap). Also here: splitting a tuple assignment into one assignment per component (Stmt.split_pair), which is how a swap reaches one parameter of an inlined call.
