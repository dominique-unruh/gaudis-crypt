    The closer on goals about values: ec_prep, ec_progress, ec_close #

    ec_values     -- a goal `∀ σ, …` about program states: every read a value, or an error
ec_prep       -- introduce, take the hypotheses apart, substitute, simplify the tests
ec_progress   -- EC's `progress`: `ec_prep`, split the goal, close what closes
ec_close      -- EC's `auto => /#`: the same, an error if a goal is left

    The closer works in two phases.

    First the states go (ec_values): a goal ∀ σ, P or ∀ σ₁ σ₂, P about program states (the side goal of a rule: the guards of an if, a conseq) is brought to the read normal form and every read is generalized to a value, as ec_skip does (Logic/ReadNorm.lean); a state that survives is an error naming the place. A goal that is not of that shape (after ec_auto or ec_skip it is already about values) is left as it is. The same is done at states bound further in (EC's final memories, P → ∀ τ₁ τ₂, frames → Q, of a while or conseq), when the introduction reaches them. A memory that is in the context (EC's &m; the other side's memory in the side goal of a one-sided rcond) is not bound by the goal: its reads, in the goal and the hypotheses, are made values after the introduction (generalize_reads_all), without the check that it does not survive.

    Then logic, on a goal about values. What auto_prep (Logic/ECClose.lean) does about states (reads through writes, frames, glob, renamings) has nothing to do there, and what is left of it is logic. ec_prep is that part alone, in auto_prep's order:

    • introduce everything: a value under the name of its binder (x, RO_m, x₁: the names the strict skip gave, usable in the proof), a hypothesis without a name;
    • take the hypotheses apart (∧, ∃, ∨), use an implication whose premise is a hypothesis (mp_hyps), substitute the equations of variables (a named one stays, subst_named), a tuple of variables by components ((p, n) = e as p = e.1, n = e.2, split_pair_eqs: what EC's sp leaves for (p, n) <- e; auto_prep does not have this step);
    • the tests back to propositions (decide … = true), indicators of events, pair equations;
    • take apart and substitute what that made.

    No step of the second phase looks at a state.

    The old tactics (auto_prep, auto_progress, auto_close) are unchanged (notes/SHA3/TacticReview.md, step 3).
