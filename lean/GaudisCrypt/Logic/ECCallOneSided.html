    EasyCrypt's one-sided call of a lossless procedure: call{1} H, call{2} H #

    prhlStmt_seq_call_left_lossless    -- `x <- M(e)` at the end of the left side only
prhlStmt_seq_call_right_lossless   -- the mirror

    EC's call{1} (H : islossless M) drops a call at the end of one side: the call terminates (H), and the postcondition must hold whatever the call returns and does to M's globals — EC's forall r (gM : glob M), Q. Here what the postcondition may rely on staying put is a frame Fr g g' between the globals before and after the call, which every run of M keeps:

    • a lens X outside M's footprint keeps its value (Module.Proc.frame_lens);
    • the glob of a finitely supported G that M stays out of keeps its content (Module.Proc.frame_glob: EC's ={glob A} against an abstract A);
    • frames combine (Module.Proc.frame_and), and True is one (Module.Proc.frame_true).

    The tactic:

    equiv_call{1} H                     -- EC's `call{1} H`, no frame
equiv_call{1} H / x₁, …, glob A     -- with the frame `x₁`, …, `glob A`

    H : islossless M may have holes (_, EC's call{1} (S_ll RO _)): they are goals after the judgment's. The frame's side conditions — lenses outside M's footprint, M outside glob A, A finitely supported — are shown by fp_disjoint and fp_finite.

    A frame against an arbitrary footprint would not be sound (CounterExamples/FramingNeedsReset.lean).
