    The abstract call rule (EasyCrypt's call (: inv) for an adversary with oracles) #

    An adversary run against two instantiations of its oracles couples with itself as EasyCrypt's abstract call rule says (equivF_abs_spec, src/phl/ecPhlFun.ml):

      equiv[ O₁.o ~ O₂.o : ={arg} ∧ inv ==> ={res} ∧ inv ]   (for every oracle o the adversary may call)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  equiv[ D(O₁) ~ D(O₂) : ={arg} ∧ ={glob D} ∧ inv ==> ={res} ∧ ={glob D} ∧ inv ]

    The invariant inv reads only the oracles' state: a lens L that the adversary is disjoint from (EasyCrypt's D <: T {-O}; EasyCrypt also rejects an inv that mentions glob D). Globals outside glob D and L are unconstrained, as in EasyCrypt.

    ={glob} is agreement on a region F (globInvF): everything the adversary may touch, and possibly more, but disjoint from L. There are three forms.

    • Procedures with holes (prhlProc_abstract_call). The adversary is A : ProcedureWithHoles holes sig, its holes are the oracle procedures it may call, and F = FVP.fvP_proc A, so ={glob} is FVP.glob A.
    • Modules (prhlProc_abstract_call_module). The adversary is a module D : Module.Arr T (procmod …), as EasyCrypt declares it, and F = FVP.fvP D. It comes with a hypothesis D.Representable ext: applied to any oracles O, D behaves like a procedure with holes filled with ext O, the procedures EasyCrypt lets D call (its OI.allowed). In EasyCrypt this holds by construction; in Gaudí it is the gap recorded as exception E2 of the SHA-3 port.
    • Any region (prhlProc_abstract_callF), which the other two instantiate.

    The proof is the oracle-agnostic machinery of Lib/RO/ROCouplingEquiv.lean (instantiate_of_fvP_gen), with two ingredients.

    • footprintCompat_globInvF: the adversary's own steps preserve the invariant. ={glob} is carried by the orbit self-coupling and inv by the per-run preservation of the L-content. It generalizes footprintCompat_PGlob from equal random-oracle tables.
    • hhole_of_prhl2: the per-oracle specifications give the per-call coupling. It generalizes ro_hhole_prhl.

    The oracle premises carry ={glob} along, as EasyCrypt's do when an oracle can use D. For oracles confined to L, prhlProc_globInvF_of_confined removes it, as EasyCrypt does. glob_set_of_le_compl rewrites ={glob} through a write that the region cannot see.

          Modules #

          EasyCrypt declares an adversary as a module of a functor type, declare module D <: T. The rule applies to such a module through a representation: a procedure with holes p that D, applied to any oracles O, behaves like when filled with ext O. ext picks the oracle procedures D may call (EasyCrypt's OI.allowed). In EasyCrypt every module is such code by construction; in Gaudí the module language is more general, and the representation is a hypothesis (Module.Representable) until a representation lemma is proven.

                Representability of concrete functor modules #

                fvP only grows when a procedure's holes are filled: a hole node contributes the write of the result and the read of the arguments, and the call that replaces it contributes those and more. So a functor module D whose application reduces to p.instantiate (ext O) (what the module command's apply_simp lemmas state) is represented by p. The footprint half goes through one oracle O₀ with an empty footprint: fvP_proc p ≤ fvP_proc (p.instantiate (ext O₀)) = fvP (D O₀) ≤ fvP D ⊔ fvP O₀ = fvP D.
